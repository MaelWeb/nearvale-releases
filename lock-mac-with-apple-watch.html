How to lock your Mac automatically when you walk away with your Apple Watch

Last updated: 2026-11-26

The short answer: macOS can unlock your Mac when your Apple Watch is nearby, but it has no setting that locks the Mac when the watch walks away. There are a few built-in ways to lock quickly, and a small class of apps that lock based on Bluetooth distance. This page explains both so you can pick what fits.

What macOS already gives you

  • Lock on demand. Press ⌃⌘Q, or choose Lock Screen from the Apple menu. It is instant, but you have to remember to do it.
  • A hot corner. In System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Hot Corners, you can set a corner to Lock Screen, so flicking the pointer there locks the Mac.
  • Lock after idle. In System Settings → Lock Screen, set the screen saver or display sleep to start after a short time and require the password immediately afterwards. This locks after you stop touching the Mac, not when you leave.
  • Unlock with Apple Watch. In System Settings → Touch ID & Password you can let a nearby, unlocked Apple Watch unlock your Mac. It only unlocks. It never locks.

Why idle timers are not enough

An idle timer protects you only after it runs out. If you set it to one minute, your screen is still readable for up to a minute after you leave, and a short timer also locks you out while you read or watch something. Most people end up choosing a long timer, which means the Mac is open exactly when you walk away to grab coffee, take a call or talk to a colleague.

Locking by distance

A proximity lock watches the Bluetooth signal of a device you carry, such as your Apple Watch, and locks the Mac when the signal gets weak. Two things are worth knowing:

  • It measures signal strength, not metres. Walls, your body and the way you hold the watch all change the reading, so a good app lets you calibrate it to your desk and lets you tell it when it locked by mistake.
  • It is a convenience, not a security boundary. Bluetooth can be blocked or the watch can be lost, so keep a strong password and FileVault on, and lock manually when it matters.
